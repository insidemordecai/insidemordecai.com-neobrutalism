October 2026 Dispatch

A quick collection of what I watched, listened to, and read this month.

31 October 2026·4 min read

On this page

  • Videos
  • Music
  • Films & Shows
  • Books
  • Podcast Episodes
  • Others

Welcome back to Dispatch, a personal roundup of videos, records, and anything that I found interesting this month. Think of Dispatch as my monthly mixtape from the internet with no real theme except “this was neat.”

Videos#

  • Key by Cars & Bids spent 72 Hours in South Dakota the incredible car culture of the place with some cool cars and picturesque roads. If you couldn't be bothered with the entire video then skip to 34:16 cause Tanner is bad ass and inspirational, PLEASE just watch that part 😂.
  • Human skill genuinely never ceases to amaze me and that was the case watching this delivery of a 500-tonne transformer in a convoy through very tight roads. It's a SLOW 20-minute video that surprisingly hooks you in but that's perhaps the ETS2 enthusiast in me speaking.
  • Once in a while you get a new channel posting an amazing first video such as Jesse Magal's case study on Lodwar town in northern Kenya. He "compares the Lodwar of ten years ago with the busy town you see today." That single video has me keen on how he improves in the coming videos.

Music#

  • Nothing But Thieves - Crazy In Love (Beyoncé cover) - NBT has quickly become one of my best discoveries this year and this cover proves why I love this band.

And here are some albums I enjoyed:

  • This Music May Contain Hope by RAYE - almost every day I have a new favourite off of the album and I think that's the hallmarks of a great album. It also helps that it's unique and playful with its theatrics.
  • Sweet Fortune by Ryan Beatty - if you loved Calico then Ryan somehow manages to follow up on that with a joyful twist.
  • Nothing But Thieves (Deluxe) - this self-titled album is so beautiful. Conor Mason reminds me of Jeff Buckley in the very best way.

Films & Shows#

Films:

  • The Furious (2025) - if you love action films then you'll definitely love this. It's action action action. We follow Wang Wei who goes against a criminal network without the help of the police in his bid to rescue his kidnapped daughter. He and a journalist deliver relentless martial arts action full of fury couple with great cinematography.
  • Beautiful Thing (1996) - this is such a beautiful and tender coming-of-age story with an amazing film look. I could watch it purely for the film visuals but its more than just that. Also, it's very British.

Shows:

  • Spider-Noir (2026) - first of all, impeccable casting with amazing acting from Nicolas Cage and the entire team. Also, this is not your typical Spider-Man story, we follow Ben Reilly, an aging private investigator, who is torn between his superhero past and his current hands off lifestyle. I didn't watch the black and white version but I hear the vibes might be better.
  • Lanterns (2026) - i don't even know how to describe this, just check out the trailer, otherwise this is the overview: "Two intergalactic cops, new recruit John Stewart and Lantern legend Hal Jordan, are drawn into a dark, Earth-based mystery as they investigate a murder in the American heartland."

Books#

After quite a couple of slow reading months, I finally finished The Odyssey by Homer (translated by Robert Fagles). The book totally lived up to the hype. I surprisingly loved its heavy reliance on flashback in the form of Odysseus telling the story of his journey. By the end of the book, I found myself more and more interested in greek mythology especially having loved The Song of Achilles by Madeline Miller a few years back. My initial goal was to finish this before the Christopher Nolan film dropped but I clearly failed at that. Nonetheless, I totally can't wait to finally watch the adaptation.

Podcast Episodes#

  • Gobi - this is such a wholesome story of a man attempting one of the toughest races in the world with the goal to win but all that changes when a furry little creature joins him. Dion and the little dog embark on journey together that basically changes their lives.
  • Jimmy Carr on What Now? with Trevor Noah - this is one of the most intellectual discussion I've heard recently which is not surprising being a podcast from Trevor Noah. It also helps that Jimmy Carr is funny, disgustingly well read and very quoteable.

Others#

  • IT Tools - this is very similar to delphi.tools that I shared in the previous dispatch but with an emphasis on your techie needs.

That’s all for this Dispatch. See you in November 🤞.
